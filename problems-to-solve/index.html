Real Problems Worth Building Around

A researched, sourced list of specific problems across Africa, not vague platitudes. Pick one, go deep, and build something that actually matters.

7 problems

Fintech

Financial Exclusion Still Locks Out a Quarter of Adults

26% of Nigerians remain formally financially excluded as of 2023, though inclusion rose from 56% to 64% since 2020 (EFInA Access to Finance Survey, 2023). Exclusion is worst in the Northeast (38%) and Northwest (47%); low or irregular income is now the top barrier, cited by 49% of the excluded, up from 31% in 2020.

Affected
Rural and northern Nigerians, informal workers with inconsistent income
Fintech / MSME

MSMEs Can't Get Credit

Only about 4% of Nigerian MSMEs have access to credit, despite MSMEs making up roughly 84–88% of the national workforce (NBS/PwC MSME surveys, exact figure contested between sources).

Affected
Micro and small business owners, the bulk of Nigeria's working population
Agriculture

Post-Harvest Losses Erase Farmer Income

Post-harvest losses in Nigeria range from 15% to over 40% depending on crop (NAERLS/UNIZIK study, 2022–2024), with yam losses in Benue State around 40% and overall annual monetary losses estimated at ₦3.5–5 trillion (ActionAid/OTACCWA). SWOFON, a farmer advocacy group, says this wipes out roughly half of smallholder income.

Affected
Smallholder farmers, especially maize, cassava, tomato, and yam growers
Healthcare

Rural Healthcare Access Lags Far Behind Urban

National doctor-to-patient ratios are disputed (estimates range from 1:2,753 to 1:10,000 depending on source), but rural-specific reporting is clearer: some southern states have one doctor per 30,000 patients, and northern states as bad as 1:45,000 (NMA president, cited in news reports). Patients in some rural areas travel 30+ km to reach care.

Affected
Rural communities, especially in the north
Education

Nigeria Has One of the World's Largest Out-of-School Populations

UNICEF's 2024 figure puts out-of-school children at 18.3 million nationally (10.2M primary-age, 8.1M secondary-age). One in four Nigerian children is out of school per UNICEF's 2024 MICS-based statement. Three northern states (Kano, Katsina, Jigawa) hold 16% of the primary-age total alone.

Affected
Children nationally, disproportionately concentrated in the north
Logistics

Last-Mile Delivery Runs on an Unverified, Informal Network

Nigeria ranks 88th of ~139 countries on the World Bank's 2023 Logistics Performance Index (behind South Africa at 19th, Egypt at 57th). A study in Ogun State found parcels routinely moved through informal transport operators without driver or agent verification, straining a system that handles ~120 million e-commerce orders yearly with no verified failed-delivery-rate data available.

Affected
E-commerce buyers and sellers, informal transport workers
Energy

Grid Power Is Unreliable Even Where It Reaches

National electricity access is 61.2% (World Bank WDI, 2023), leaving Nigeria with the highest number of people without grid access of any country (~85–86 million). The national grid collapsed at least 12 times in 2024 alone, and the World Bank estimates unreliable power costs Nigeria ~2% of GDP.

Affected
Households and businesses nationwide, urban and rural alike
